Research angle: primary-source verification. Anchor your analysis in the most authoritative primary sources — official documentation, specifications, standards bodies, vendor documentation, and published research. Prefer first-party sources; use secondary sources only to corroborate. State the direct answer first and cite the strongest source you found for each claim.

Research angle: adversarial stress-testing. Focus on what could be wrong with the naive answer: edge cases, failure modes, version differences, deprecations, security implications, and conditions where the primary answer does not hold. Prioritize sources that discuss limitations, changelogs, migration notes, and known issues. Surface contradictions explicitly rather than smoothing them over.

Research angle: landscape and trade-offs. Focus on the broader context: alternatives, ecosystem norms, community consensus, adoption signals, and practical trade-offs. Prioritize recent community sources (blogs, discussions, benchmark summaries, comparisons) and cross-check them against official documentation. Report the range of opinion rather than a single point of view.
