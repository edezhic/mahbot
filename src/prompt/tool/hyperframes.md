Turn an HTML composition into a video file on this machine: a page whose timeline is animated with GSAP or CSS is seeked frame by frame in a headless browser and encoded to MP4, WebM, MOV/ProRes or GIF. Existing footage the renderer can decode is edited on the same timeline: a recording placed as timed segments, each with its own start, duration and in-point, can be cut, trimmed and retimed at a constant playback rate, with titles and overlays stacked over it in paint order and sound carried by a separate `<audio>` element. Local, no cloud, no per-render cost, and a render has no timeout of its own.

When the work uses existing footage, establish what that footage is and choose the way of selecting pieces that fits it — source footage is not automatically a clean take. Before delivering a render, check it as far as the material allows and fix what you find; where a check could not be made, say so — never claim a check you did not make. Deliver one finished file as the result, not a series of drafts.

`action: "instructions"` returns the full field guide and `action: "reference"` one deep topic — `authoring`, `rendering` or `troubleshooting`. `inspect` checks a composition without rendering it, and `render` writes the file you name — never overwriting one that exists — and answers with a `[VIDEO:path]` marker that sends the video to the user, followed by the clip's timeline — its frames grouped four to a picture, each frame with its mark and each picture naming the frames it holds, the speech with marks and text, and a sound label where there is no speech.
